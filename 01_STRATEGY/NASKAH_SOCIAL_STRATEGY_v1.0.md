# 2. BRAND POSITIONING

## A. What Naskah Is

Naskah is an **academic support platform** that helps students, researchers, and academic professionals navigate the research and writing process with clarity, confidence, and integrity.

Naskah operates as a **trusted academic companion** — part strategist, part educator, part practical guide.

Naskah is not defined by any single product. Naskah is defined by the **relationship it builds with people going through the academic process.**

## B. Who Naskah Serves

Primary: Students facing the research/thesis process — particularly those who are confused, stuck, overwhelmed, or afraid of making mistakes.

Secondary: Early-career researchers, young academics, and health professionals who need practical research support.

Tertiary: Anyone intellectually curious about research methodology, statistics, journals, and academic writing.

## C. What Problem Naskah Solves

The core problem is not a single technical gap.

The core problem is the **gap between what the academic process demands and what most students feel equipped to handle** — emotionally, practically, and intellectually.

Specifically:
- Not knowing where to start
- Not understanding methodology
- Not knowing which statistical test to use
- Not knowing how to find or read journals
- Not knowing how to prepare for seminars
- Not knowing how to use AI responsibly in research
- Feeling isolated, scared, and overwhelmed in the process

## D. Why Naskah Should Be Trusted

Naskah earns trust by:

1. **Demonstrating expertise** — Content that shows deep understanding of research, methodology, statistics, and academic writing
2. **Being honest** — Not overpromising, not fabricating, not exaggerating
3. **Being practical** — Giving actionable guidance, not just theory
4. **Being human** — Acknowledging the emotional reality of academic work
5. **Being consistent** — Showing up regularly with quality
6. **Being transparent** — Showing how Naskah works (Behind Naskah / Naskah Inside)
7. **Providing proof** — Case studies, before/after, real outcomes (ethically shared)

## E. What Makes Naskah Different

Naskah differentiates by:

- **Not sounding like a textbook** — Conversational, relatable, practical
- **Not sounding like a sales page** — Educational-first, trust-building
- **Not sounding like generic motivation** — Specifically grounded in research/academic reality
- **Combining emotional intelligence with technical competence** — Smart without being intimidating
- **Using AI strategically and transparently** — Not hiding behind it, not pretending it doesn't exist
- **Thinking in systems** — Not just isolated posts, but a learning content system

## F. What Naskah Should NOT Be Positioned As

- A "jasa skripsi" (thesis mill)
- An academic fraud service
- A generic "jasa penulisan" (writing service) without differentiation
- A motivational account
- A meme account
- An AI slop account
- A textbook
- A know-it-all authority that intimidates

# 4. CONTENT STRATEGY

## Pillar 01 — ACADEMIC EDUCATION (25%)

### Purpose
Build **authority and educational value.** Demonstrate that Naskah deeply understands research, methodology, statistics, and academic writing.

### Audience Problem
"I don't understand [concept]. Can someone explain it clearly?"

### Content Examples
- What is cross-sectional vs longitudinal?
- When to use Chi-Square vs t-test?
- How to read a forest plot
- Understanding P-value intuitively
- Difference between OR and RR
- How to determine sample size
- How to write a good research question

### Preferred Platform
Instagram (carousels, static explainers), Threads (short educational insights)

### Desired Audience Behavior
Save → Share → Follow → Trust Naskah's expertise

### Business Role
Authority building. The more people associate Naskah with clarity on hard topics, the more likely they trust Naskah when they need help.

### Recommended Frequency
Consistent presence. This is the backbone pillar — should appear in every week.

---

## PILLAR 02 — ACADEMIC PAIN / RELATABILITY (15%)

### Purpose
Build **emotional connection and reach.** Show that Naskah understands what it FEELS LIKE to go through the academic process.

### Audience Problem
"I feel alone in this struggle. Does anyone else feel this way?"

### Content Examples
- "Dosen bilang revisinya sedikit." (Dosen's 'small' revision = 17 pages)
- When you finally understand your methodology after 3 weeks
- The moment your proposal gets approved
- Staring at SPSS output and understanding nothing
- "I'll start writing tomorrow" — said for 47 days

### Preferred Platform
Threads (conversational, relatable, witty), Instagram (relatable carousels, reels)

### Desired Audience Behavior
Relate → Comment → Share → Follow (for the feeling of being understood)

### Business Role
Reach and connection. This pillar brings people in who might not actively search for methodology content but feel immediately seen.

### Recommended Frequency
Regular but not dominant. 1-2x per week on Threads, occasionally on Instagram.

### Caution
Do not let this pillar reduce Naskah to a meme/relatable account. Relatability must serve the larger trust strategy.

# 5. INSTAGRAM STRATEGY

## Positioning

Instagram is Naskah's **primary knowledge showcase and discovery engine.**

It is where Naskah demonstrates competence, provides value, builds visual credibility, and converts interest into trust and action.

Instagram should feel:
**Clean. Useful. Saveable. Smart. Visually consistent.**

## Audience Behavior on Instagram

The audience arrives from:
- Explore/discovery (hashtag, algorithm, shares)
- Profile visits (from Threads, DMs, word-of-mouth)
- Saves from friends
- Reel discovery

Typical behavior flow:
```
Discover → Scan → Recognize value → Save → Follow → Return for more → Eventually inquire
```

Instagram users **save what they might need later.** This is Naskah's superpower — academic content is inherently saveable.

## Content Priorities

Ranked by strategic value:

1. **Educational carousels** — Highest save/share potential. Foundation of authority.
2. **Practical guides** — High utility. Directly useful. Drives saves and shares.
3. **Relatable content** — Drives reach and comments. Brings new audience in.
4. **Case studies / proof** — Drives trust and conversion.
5. **Thought leadership** — Drives comments and follows.
6. **Behind the scenes** — Drives trust and transparency.
7. **Product/offer** — Drives conversion. Must never dominate.

## Format Priorities

### Carousel
- Primary workhorse format
- Ideal for: step-by-step guides, methodology explainers, checklists, comparisons, statistical concepts
- Target: 7-10 slides with clear title, progression, and closing CTA
- Each slide should work as a standalone insight while contributing to a coherent narrative

### Reel
- Discovery driver. Reels bring new people in.
- Ideal for: quick tips, common mistakes, methodology snippets, relatable moments
- Target: 15-60 seconds. Hook within first 2 seconds.
- Do not sacrifice substance for style

### Static Post
- Quick insights, quotes, single-concept explanations
- Lower production effort, lower save potential
- Use for maintaining consistency on lighter days

### Story
- Behind-the-scenes, polls, questions, casual updates, content teasers
- Engagement driver
- Lower production barrier

### Case Study
- Anonymized client stories
- Before/after framing
- Problem → Process → Outcome

## Hook Style for Instagram

Instagram hooks must work visually AND textually.

The first 1-2 seconds of a reel or the first slide of a carousel must create:
- **Curiosity** — "I need to know what comes next"
- **Relevance** — "This is about MY problem"
- **Clarity** — "I know what I'll get from this"

Examples:
- "Kalau kamu pakai Chi-Square hanya karena katanya paling umum, baca ini dulu."
- "5 langkah sebelum seminar proposal. Yang nomor 3 sering dilupakan."
- "Jurnal yang kamu baca sebenarnya tidak berkualitas. Begini cara mengeceknya."

## CTA Strategy for Instagram

Use only ONE CTA per post. Choose based on objective:

| Objective | CTA Example |
|---|---|
| Save | "Save post ini kalau nanti kamu masuk tahap analisis data." |
| Share | "Share ke teman yang sedang skripsi." |
| Follow | "Follow Naskah kalau kamu mau belajar penelitian tanpa bahasa yang bikin pusing." |
| Comment | "Cerita di bawah — tahap mana yang paling bikin kamu stuck?" |
| Conversion | "Kalau kamu ada di tahap ini dan butuh bantuan, DM Naskah." |

## Visual Expectations

Visual must be:
- **Clean and uncluttered**
- **Consistent** (colors, fonts, layout patterns)
- **Readable** (large enough text, good contrast)
- **Branded** (logo/watermark placement consistent)
- **Educational-looking** (not lifestyle, not overly decorative)

Visual must NOT be:
- Chaotic
- Text-heavy without structure
- Low contrast
- Stock photo dependent
- Visually inconsistent between posts

## Success Signals for Instagram

| Signal | What It Indicates |
|---|---|
| High saves | Content is useful and relevant to real needs |
| High shares | Content is so useful people want others to see it |
| Profile visits → follows | Audience is interested in more |
| DM inquiries | Trust is converting to action |
| Consistent reach | Algorithm and audience alignment |
| Repeat engagement | Audience loyalty forming |

# 6. THREADS STRATEGY

## Positioning

Threads is Naskah's **personality and conversation layer.**

It is where Naskah shows its human side, starts conversations, builds community feeling, and distributes insights in a more informal format.

Threads should feel:
**Conversational. Smart. Opinionated. Relatable. Authentic. Not trying too hard.**

## Audience Behavior on Threads

The audience arrives from:
- Threads algorithm/discovery
- Profile link from Instagram
- Cross-platform sharing
- Organic conversation threads

Typical behavior flow:
```
Read → Relate → Reply → Follow → Remember → Return for conversation
```

Threads users engage through **reading and replying.** The currency is conversation, not saves.

## Content Priorities

1. **Educational insights** — Short, punchy, designed to make people think "oh, I didn't know that"
2. **Relatable observations** — Academic life humor and reality
3. **Opinion/thought leadership** — What does Naskah think about [topic]?
4. **Discussion starters** — Questions that invite genuine response
5. **Myth-busting** — Correcting common misconceptions
6. **Mini-stories** — Short narrative posts about academic experiences
7. **Quick practical tips** — Actionable advice in 2-3 sentences

## Format Priorities

### Short Insight Post (1-3 sentences)
- Quick hit of knowledge
- "P-value bukan probabilitas hipotesis nol benar. P-value adalah probabilitas mendapat data setidaknya selinik ini jika hipotesis nol benar."
- High impression potential, low production effort

### Educational Thread (multi-post)
- Longer format for deeper explanation
- Suitable for methodology breakdowns, statistical concepts
- Requires stronger opening hook

### Relatable Post
- Short, witty, observational
- "Skripsi chapter 2 is not a chapter. It's a lifestyle."
- Drives replies and quote posts

### Discussion Starter
- "What's the most confusing methodology concept you've encountered? Let's break it down."
- Drives replies and community feeling

### Opinion Post
- Clear stance with reasoning
- "AI won't replace researchers. But researchers who use AI will outperform those who don't."
- Drives engagement and brand identity

### Myth-Busting
- "Mitos: Semua penelitian harus menggunakan kuantitatif."
- Drives replies, shares, and authority

## Conversation Strategy for Threads

1. **Reply to replies.** When someone engages, respond. This builds community.
2. **Ask follow-up questions.** Turn one reply into a conversation.
3. **Quote-post your own insights** when a reply inspires a deeper thought.
4. **Never be defensive.** If challenged, respond with evidence and calm.
5. **Acknowledge when you're wrong.** This builds extraordinary trust.

## Tone for Threads

More informal than Instagram, but still:
- **Truthful** — No fabricated claims
- **Useful** — Every post should teach or clarify something
- **Brand-safe** — No offensive content, no academic fraud endorsement
- **Intellectually responsible** — Opinions backed by reasoning

Threads can be:
- Witty ("Di dunia skripsi, revisi adalah satu-satunya konstanta.")
- Provocating ("Jurnal yang kamu baca tanpa memahami statistiknya = kamu membaca fiksi.")
- Casual ("btw kalau ada yang masih bingung soal beda cross-sectional sama cohort, ini singkatnya...")
- Self-aware ("Kami tahu 90% dari konten ini cuma akan di-save dan nggak dibaca. Tapi nggak apa-apa, yang penting nanti pas kamu butuh, ada.")

## CTA Strategy for Threads

Threads CTAs should be lighter and more conversational:

| Objective | CTA Example |
|---|---|
| Reply | "Pernah ngalamin ini? Cerita di bawah." |
| Follow | "Follow kalau kamu mau insight penelitian yang nggak bikin pusing." |
| Share/Repost | "Repost kalau ini bermanfaat buat temanmu yang sedang skripsi." |
| Soft conversion | "Kalau kamu ada di situasi ini, Naskah bisa bantu. DM aja." |

## Success Signals for Threads

| Signal | What It Indicates |
|---|---|
| Replies | Conversation is happening, community forming |
| Quote posts | Insight is being shared and discussed |
| Follows from Threads | Content is bringing people into the ecosystem |
| Conversation quality | People are asking real questions, not just reacting |
| Repeat engagement | Community is forming, not just passing through |

## Cross-Platform Rules

### Shared Across Both Platforms
- Brand voice fundamentals (smart, human, helpful, honest, calm, practical)
- Truth and accuracy standards
- Ethical boundaries
- Core messaging about Naskah's positioning
- Key educational insights (repurposed, not duplicated)

### Platform-Native
| Element | Instagram | Threads |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | Structured, visual-first | Conversational, text-first |
| Length | Designed for scanning | Designed for reading |
| Humor | Visual, meme-like | Witty, observational |
| Depth | Carousel can be deep | Usually shorter, occasionally threaded |
| CTA | Save/Share focused | Reply/Follow focused |
| Engagement metric | Saves, shares | Replies, reposts |

### Repurposing Rule
One insight can exist on both platforms, but must be **adapted in format, language, and structure.** Never copy-paste a caption from Instagram to Threads or vice versa.

# 8. CONTENT ECOSYSTEM

## The Atom Principle

Every valuable insight is a **content atom** that can be split into multiple formats.

```
CORE INSIGHT
│
├── Threads post (short, punchy)
├── Threads thread (deeper explanation)
├── IG Carousel (visual, structured, saveable)
├── IG Reel (short, engaging, discoverable)
├── IG Story (behind-the-scenes or teaser)
├── FAQ (evergreen reference)
├── Case Study (when evidence exists)
└── Product Education (when relevant to services)
```

## Decision: Which Format Should an Insight Become?

### Choose THREADS when:
- The insight is short and conversational
- The insight is opinion-based
- The insight invites discussion
- The insight is best as text without visual structure
- You want to test audience interest before investing in visual production

### Choose INSTAGRAM CAROUSEL when:
- The insight has 3+ sequential points
- The insight benefits from visual structure
- The insight is highly saveable
- The insight needs to demonstrate visual competence
- The insight is actionable/checklist-like

### Choose INSTAGRAM REEL when:
- The insight can be delivered in under 60 seconds
- The insight benefits from visual demonstration
- You want maximum algorithmic discovery
- The insight is surprising or counter-intuitive
- The hook is strong enough for 2-second attention

### Choose INSTAGRAM STORY when:
- The insight is casual or behind-the-scenes
- You want to gauge interest (polls, questions)
- The insight is a teaser for longer content
- The insight is timely/temporary

### Choose FAQ when:
- The insight addresses a frequently asked question
- The insight should be referenceable and evergreen

### Choose CASE STUDY when:
- You have real evidence to share
- The insight benefits from proof

### Choose PRODUCT EDUCATION when:
- The insight connects to a specific Naskah service
- The audience has already built trust

## Repurposing Workflow

```
1. Identify core insight
2. Assess: Is this insight strong enough for multiple formats?
3. If yes: Create the PRIMARY format first
4. Assess: Does this insight have secondary format potential?
5. If yes: Adapt (never duplicate) for secondary platform
6. Track: Which format performed best for this type of insight?
7. Learn: Use data to inform future format decisions
```

## Anti-Pattern

Do NOT:
- Create a carousel and copy-paste it as a Threads post
- Create a reel script and turn it into a carousel without adapting
- Create 5 versions of the same insight in one week

Do:
- Create one excellent primary format
- Adapt for one secondary format if the insight warrants it
- Space repurposed content by at least 3-5 days

# 10. CONTENT DECISION ENGINE

## Decision Framework: "Should We Create This?"

```
INPUT: Idea or request

STEP 1: AUDIENCE NEED
Does this address a real audience problem or need?
→ If NO: REJECT or REFRAME
→ If YES: Continue

STEP 2: STRATEGIC RELEVANCE
Does this align with current strategic priorities?
→ If NO: DEFER or REFRAME
→ If YES: Continue

STEP 3: BRAND FIT
Does this sound/look/feel like Naskah?
→ If NO: REFRAME or REJECT
→ If YES: Continue

STEP 4: VALUE
Does this provide genuine value to the audience?
→ If NO: REJECT
→ If YES: Continue

STEP 5: PLATFORM FIT
Which platform does this belong on?
→ If clearly Instagram: Continue as Instagram
→ If clearly Threads: Continue as Threads
→ If both: Choose the STRONGEST fit first
→ If neither: REJECT

STEP 6: TIMING
Is this timely or evergreen?
→ If timely: Is the window still open?
→ If evergreen: Is now a good time relative to recent content?

STEP 7: BUSINESS RELEVANCE
Does this contribute to business goals (directly or indirectly)?
→ Track but don't force. Not every post needs business relevance.

STEP 8: FINAL DECISION
Should we create this?
→ PUBLISH (after QC)
→ DEFER (right idea, wrong time)
→ REFRAME (right value, wrong angle/format)
→ REJECT (does not meet quality/strategic threshold)
```

## Decision: Instagram or Threads?

```
Is the content visual-heavy?
→ YES → Instagram
→ NO → Continue

Does it require structured, sequential explanation?
→ YES → Instagram (carousel)
→ NO → Continue

Is it conversational or opinion-based?
→ YES → Threads
→ NO → Continue

Is it a quick insight or observation?
→ YES → Threads
→ NO → Continue

Does it invite discussion?
→ YES → Threads (possibly also Instagram)
→ NO → Continue

Does it need to demonstrate visual brand competence?
→ YES → Instagram
→ NO → Continue
```

## Decision: Evergreen or Timely?

**Evergreen:**
- Methodology explanations
- Statistical concepts
- Research process guides
- Common mistakes
- Tool tutorials
- General advice

**Timely:**
- Academic calendar events (seminar season, thesis deadlines)
- Trending academic discussions
- Current events related to research
- Campaign-specific content
- Trending formats or audio

**Evergreen content can be recycled. Timely content has an expiration date.**

## Decision: Single Post or Series?

**Make it a SERIES when:**
- The topic is too large for one post
- There's clear audience demand for more
- It could become a recurring content property
- The first post performed well and demand exists for continuation

**Keep it as a SINGLE POST when:**
- The insight is complete in one post
- There's no demonstrated demand for more
- The topic is narrow enough for one piece
- Creating a series would feel forced

## Decision: Is This Worth Publishing?

Final gate. Ask:

1. **Would I save this?** (If not, why would anyone else?)
2. **Would I share this?** (If not, it lacks shareability)
3. **Does this make Naskah more trusted?** (If not, what's the point?)
4. **Is the hook honest?** (If not, rewrite)
5. **Is the content true?** (If uncertain, verify or remove the claim)
6. **Does it pass QC?** (If not, revise before publishing)
7. **Is there a clear reason for this to exist?** (If not, don't publish)

# 12. EXPERIMENTATION FRAMELOGY

## Purpose

Every week, at least one content piece should test a hypothesis. Over time, this builds an evidence base for what works for Naskah's specific audience.

## Variables That Can Be Tested

| Variable | What It Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Opening line/approach | Question vs statement vs bold claim |
| Topic | Content subject area | Statistics vs methodology vs relatable |
| Format | Content type | Carousel vs reel vs static vs thread |
| Length | Content depth | Short (3 slides) vs long (10 slides) |
| CTA | Call to action type | Save vs share vs comment vs follow |
| Tone | Voice approach | Formal vs casual vs witty |
| Posting time | When content goes live | Morning vs afternoon vs evening |
| Educational depth | How technical the content gets | Introductory vs intermediate vs advanced |
| Visual style | Design approach | Minimal vs detailed vs text-heavy |

## Experiment Design Principles

### 1. One Variable at a Time
When testing a hook, keep topic, format, and CTA constant. Otherwise, you can't attribute results.

### 2. Minimum Sample
Do not draw conclusions from a single post. Minimum evidence requirements:

- **Pattern detection:** 3+ similar results
- **Hypothesis confirmation:** 5+ data points
- **Strategy change:** Repeated evidence across multiple weeks

### 3. Control Group Concept
Compare new approaches against your baseline. If your average carousel gets 500 saves, and the new approach gets 700, that's meaningful. If it gets 520, it's noise.

### 4. Document Everything
Every experiment should be logged:

```
EXPERIMENT LOG
Date:
Variable tested:
Hypothesis:
Content:
Result:
Comparison to baseline:
Learning:
Decision: ADOPT / MODIFY / ABANDON / TEST FURTHER
```

## What NOT to Conclude

- "This post failed" → Instead: "This approach didn't work in this context. Why?"
- "Carousels are better than reels" → Instead: "For this topic, this carousel outperformed this reel."
- "Posting at 9am is best" → Instead: "The last 3 posts at 9am outperformed the last 3 at 3pm."
- "This topic doesn't work" → Instead: "This topic with this hook and format didn't work."

## Experimentation Calendar

| Week | Experiment Focus | Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1-2 | Hook styles | Specific hooks outperform generic hooks |
| Week 3-4 | Format test | Carousel vs reel for same topic |
| Week 5-6 | CTA test | Save-focused vs share-focused |
| Week 7-8 | Tone test | Formal vs casual for educational content |
| Week 9-10 | Length test | Short carousel vs long carousel |
| Week 11-12 | Topic test | Education vs relatable for reach |

**Rotate experiments. Never test everything at once.**
